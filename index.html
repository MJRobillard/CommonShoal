The mechanic

Small beats sprawling

01

Close a loop

Your boat's track is the pen. Circle a stretch of water — around a shoal, a channel edge, wherever you think the bottom's changed — and closing the loop claims it.

02

Small beats sprawling

A tight, precise claim can't be swallowed by a bigger loop drawn around it — at most, the big loop takes the open water around your ground, never the ground itself. Trace tighter than an existing claim and you carve that piece out for yourself, at reduced points.

03

Holds until it's proven wrong

Claims run on a long clock, but any boater who finds open water where a claim says there's a bar can report it — enough reports and the ground opens back up. That's how the map tracks a bar that's actually moved.

Why it matters

The chart is always a little bit wrong

NOAA still charts this stretch on sheet 12266 — a shoal off Benoni Point at the river's mouth, abrupt shoaling up at Easton Point — but soundings get revised on a survey's schedule, not the tide's. Sandbars here shift with every strong blow, and finding out the hard way is how keels get bent: grounding is one of the most common ways a good day on the Chesapeake goes wrong, right alongside falling overboard and capsizing. The people who already know the current truth are out there every week — hobbyist boaters, crabbers, the sailing club. This just gives that knowledge a place to live, updated by the hour instead of by the season.

None of this is monetized. No ads, no paid tiers, no data resale — the map exists because the community needs it and keeps it honest.

Sources: NOAA Coast Pilot 3, 2023 USCG Recreational Boating Statistics.

NOAA already runs a water-level monitor at this river's mouth and named the Choptank complex a federal Habitat Focus Area back in 2014. A live, crowdsourced shoal map is exactly the kind of dataset that effort can't generate on its own, but could absolutely put to use.
Open by design

How the commons stays a commons

  • Code is open source on GitHub — anyone can read it, fork it, or point out what's broken.
  • Sandbar data is released under an open geodata license, so no one, including us, can lock it up later.
  • No monetization: the pub tab and any prizes come from local sponsorship, not user data.
  • Every contributor's loops are attributed to them, on the map, permanently.
  • Decisions about the project happen in the open — issues, not backroom calls.
  • Built to hand off: if a local group wants to steward this long-term, it's built to be handed over.
